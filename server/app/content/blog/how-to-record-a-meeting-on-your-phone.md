To record a meeting on your phone and end up with usable notes, ask everyone's permission first, put the phone on the table close to the speakers, start recording before anyone talks, and keep it running until the end. Then turn the recording into a transcript and a short list of decisions and actions the same day, while people still remember what was meant.

The recorder button is the easy part. The quality of your notes is decided before you press it.

## Ask first

Recording-consent laws vary by country and state. Some places need only your own consent, others need everyone's. You can't be expected to know the law of every place you work, so the simple rule is: **ask everyone before you record.** Say it out loud at the start.

Something like: "I'd like to record this so I can write accurate notes. It stays on my phone and I'll delete it afterwards. Is everyone fine with that?" Then wait for answers. If someone says no, don't record.

This is not legal advice. If your meeting is sensitive, check with someone qualified. Asking also changes the room for the better: people know what the recording is for, and it stops being a surprise later.

## Place the phone well

Most poor transcripts come from poor placement, not poor software.

- **Put it in the middle of the table, microphone side up.** Phones usually have a microphone at the bottom edge; don't cover it with a notebook or a hand.
- **Keep it close to the people talking.** Distance is the biggest enemy. A phone two metres away from the quietest speaker will lose them.
- **Keep it off anything that vibrates.** A laptop being typed on or a table you drum on shows up as noise. A folded napkin or a notebook underneath helps.
- **Keep it clear of walls and corners, where voices bounce back.** In a larger group, a phone near the middle beats one at the edge.
- **Turn off notifications and set Do Not Disturb.** A buzz can drown a sentence.

## Handle the room

You can't fix a room, but you can nudge it.

**Close the door and the window.** Corridor noise and traffic compete with voices.

**Avoid hard, echoing spaces if you can.** Glass-walled rooms echo; a room with soft furnishings gives clearer speech.

**Ask people not to talk over each other.** Overlapping speech is the hardest thing for any transcriber and the hardest for a human to minute. A gentle "one at a time, please" at the start works.

**Say names early.** If people introduce themselves, the speaker labels are easier to match to real people afterwards.

## Long meetings and keeping it recording

**Check storage and battery before you start.** A long recording needs space, and a flat phone ends your notes.

**Plug in for anything over an hour.**

**Keep recording while you do other things.** Verbale carries on recording with the screen off, while you use other apps, and in a small floating window (picture-in-picture), so you can check a document without ending the recording. Whatever you use, test this before the meeting, because not every recorder does it.

**Expect processing to take time.** On a phone, a long meeting takes a while to turn into notes. Verbale does this in the background and sends a notification when the notes are ready.

You will not remember everything that mattered. When something important is said, a decision or a commitment, mark it. ## After the meeting

Do these within a day.

1. **Turn the recording into text.** A transcript separated by speaker is far easier to scan than raw audio.
2. **Pull out decisions, actions and open questions.** The [minutes of meeting template](/blog/minutes-of-meeting-template) gives you a structure to follow.
3. **Check the draft against the audio.** Where a line matters, listen to it. Tapping a line in Verbale plays that moment.
4. **Send it out.** Minutes sent the same day get read and acted on.
5. **Delete what you no longer need.** The fewer copies of a recording, the better, especially for sensitive meetings.

## FAQ

### Is it legal to record a meeting on my phone?

It depends on where you are and who is in the meeting. Recording-consent laws vary by country and state, and some require everyone's permission. The safe habit is to ask everyone first. For anything sensitive, take proper advice.

### Where should I put my phone to record a meeting?

In the middle of the table, close to the people speaking, with the microphone uncovered and away from anything that vibrates. Distance to the speakers matters more than anything else.

### Will recording drain my battery?

Recording is fairly light. Turning a long recording into notes on the phone is heavier, so plug in if the meeting is long.

### Can I keep recording if I switch apps or lock the screen?

That depends on the app, so test whichever one you use before an important meeting. The section on keeping it recording says what to check.
